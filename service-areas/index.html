Coverage is a statement about where we work, not a list for search engines

Arco Outdoors works across Broward, Miami-Dade and Palm Beach counties, from a business address in Davie. That is the whole geographic claim, and it is deliberately modest — you will not find forty municipality names stacked at the bottom of this page.

There is a reason contractors do that, and it is not to help you. A long list of cities is cheap to type and reads as reach. What it actually tells a homeowner is nothing: not whether the contractor has ever driven to that city, not whether they understand what the housing stock there is like, and not whether they know which office reviews the permit.

So the eight markets below are the ones we build in often enough to write something useful about. Each has its own page, and each of those pages says something different, because the cities genuinely differ — in how old the houses are, how big and what shape the lots are, whether the property is exposed to coastal air, whether an association has a view on your fence, and which municipality reviews the work.

Priority markets

Eight markets, and what tends to come up in each

Character notes, not sales copy. Each is about the properties we see, and none of them asserts a completed project in that city.

Parkland

Broward's north-western corner, largely master-planned and comparatively recent, with generous rear yards and a low-density, tree-lined character. Whole-yard programmes come up here more than single surfaces.

Davie

Our own address, and the most varied property mix on this list — acreage and equestrian land, older ranch houses, standard subdivisions and newer zero-lot-line developments, sometimes within a mile of each other.

Weston

A planned city of consistent communities, water throughout, and association guidelines that are part of nearly every conversation. Yards are usually well-built and uniform, which is exactly what people want changed.

Plantation

One of Broward's established cities, with older housing stock and a genuine tree canopy. Most work here is replacing something that is already there and negotiating with roots and shade while doing it.

Fort Lauderdale

Coastal and waterway exposure, and the tightest sites on this list. Access, salt air and compact yards drive most of the decisions, and material and hardware specification matters more the closer you are to the water.

Pembroke Pines

A broad spread of subdivision housing across a large area, with plenty of standard rear yards where a patio, a turf panel and a fence line are the whole brief.

Coral Springs

Suburban family yards immediately south of Parkland, frequently with a pool already in the ground, so pool surrounds, play-safe surfaces and enclosures come up constantly.

Boca Raton

Our Palm Beach County market, spanning inland communities and properties near the coast — which means the permitting authority and the exposure both change depending on where in the city you are.

All eight now have a page

Each one is written around what that market actually presents — access and salt near the coast, community approvals in the planned cities, roots and older surfaces in the established ones. None of them is the same page with the city name changed, which is why there are eight rather than thirty.

The wider picture

Three counties, from one address

Broward is the core. Seven of the eight markets above sit in it, and so does our own address. It is where we work most, and where scheduling several sites in a week is straightforward rather than something that has to be engineered.

Palm Beach County to the north is served through Boca Raton and the communities around it. It is a different permitting jurisdiction from Broward, which matters more to your project than the drive does.

Miami-Dade to the south is the third county in our coverage. It is the furthest reach for a business working out of Davie, and it is where the honest answer about a project sometimes depends on the size of the job as much as its location.

What we deliberately do not publish is a service radius in miles. A radius sounds precise and decides nothing: a substantial project at the edge of the map can be entirely sensible, and a very small job much closer to home may not be. Scope, access and schedule are what actually determine whether we are the right contractor for a given property, so those are what we talk about when you call.

Why the pages differ

What actually changes from one city to the next

These are the variables that make a location page worth writing. If none of them applied, one page would do for the whole county — and a lot of contractor websites are quietly admitting exactly that.

How old the houses are

A city built mostly in the 1990s presents different work from one built mostly in the 1960s. Newer stock means original surfaces reaching the end of their first life; older stock means replacing what has already been replaced once, and finding out what is underneath.

Lot size and shape

Generous rear yards invite zoning and phasing. Zero-lot-line and narrow side yards invite precision, and rule out some machinery entirely. The same service is a different job on each.

Exposure

Salt-laden air near the coast changes hardware, fixings and finishes. Inland, it is sun, humidity and driving rain doing the work. Both are real; they are not the same specification.

Trees

A mature canopy shades a yard, drops debris on it and puts roots under it. Cities with an established canopy produce paving and drainage problems that a newer neighbourhood simply does not have yet.

Who reviews the work

Permitting is municipal and county business, and requirements differ between neighbouring cities. Which counter your project goes to is decided by your address, and it is confirmed per project rather than assumed.

Whether an association is involved

In some of these markets almost every property is inside a community with its own approval process; in others it is the exception. It changes the sequence of a project and usually the timeline.

Wherever you are

How a first visit works

  1. You describe the property

    Where it is, roughly what you are thinking about, and anything you already know is awkward — a slope, a tree, standing water, an association, a neighbour. Enough for us to say honestly whether we are the right contractor before either of us spends an afternoon on it.

  2. We come and look

    On site, at the property, because levels, drainage, access and the condition of what is already there cannot be assessed any other way. This is also where the constraints specific to your address get raised.

  3. Requirements get confirmed

    Which municipality reviews the work, what it requires for this scope, and whether an association approval sits alongside it. Confirmed for your property rather than assumed from a neighbouring city.

  4. You get it in writing

    Scope, materials, what is included, what is being submitted for permit, and what is not in the proposal. If something about your site makes part of the idea unwise, that is in there too.

Common questions

Questions about where we work

Are the eight cities listed here the only places you work?

No. They are the markets we build in most often, so they are the ones we have written pages about. The wider coverage is Broward, Miami-Dade and Palm Beach counties. If your address is in those counties and it is not on this page, that is a gap in our writing rather than a gap in our coverage — call and ask, and you will get a straight answer about whether the project makes sense for us.

Have you completed projects in my city before?

Ask us directly and we will tell you. What we will not do is publish a claim on this website that we have built in a particular city, because a claim like that should be backed by a verifiable project rather than by a page that needed a local reference. Several contractor websites list every municipality in the county alongside photographs that were taken somewhere else entirely. We would rather answer the question in person — the Projects page explains what we will publish, and when.

Does the city my house is in change what I can build?

It can. Permitting is handled by the municipality and the county, and what is required, what has to be submitted and how it is inspected differ between them. Neighbouring cities are not interchangeable on this. Rather than publish rules that may not apply to your address, we confirm the requirements for your specific property as part of the project and handle the process for the work we carry out.

Do you work with homeowner associations?

Yes, and in several of these markets it is a routine part of the job rather than an exception. Association approval is a separate track from municipal permitting and often the slower one, so it is worth raising at the first conversation. If you have your community's guidelines to hand, bring them to the consultation — they frequently narrow the material and colour choices before anything else does.

How does distance from Davie affect a project?

It affects logistics rather than standards. Travel time influences how a schedule is planned, how deliveries and site visits are grouped, and occasionally whether a small job on its own is practical a long way out. It does not change how the work is built or what is written in the proposal. If distance genuinely affects what we can commit to on your project, we will say so at the consultation rather than after.

What happens at the first visit?

We come to the property, because none of the things that decide an outdoor project can be established from a satellite image: how the ground actually falls, where water goes after a storm, what the existing surfaces are doing, how the house opens onto the yard and where the access for machinery and materials is. That visit is also where the constraints get raised — permitting, associations, easements, trees — before any of them can turn into a surprise.

Not sure whether we cover you?

Tell us the address and what you are thinking about. If your property is a good fit we will say so, and if it is not, we will say that instead of driving out to find a way to make it one.
