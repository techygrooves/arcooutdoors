What happens next

1. We reply and ask anything the form did not cover.

2. A free visit to the property — levels, drainage, access and existing conditions get looked at, because none of them can be judged from photographs.

3. A written scope: what is included, what is excluded, and which elements are performed directly rather than coordinated.

No pressure to decide on the day, and no discount that expires when we leave.

What we ask for, and what we do not

The form asks for a name, two ways to reach you, the ZIP code the work is in, and what you want done. That is everything needed to give a useful first answer.

It does not ask for your street address, your budget, or any financial detail. Please do not send account numbers or documents — nothing at this stage needs them.

Before you send it

What you are actually asking for

Licensed contractor

Florida Certified Building Contractor licence CBC1269393, published in the footer of every page here so it can be checked against the state record before you let anyone onto your property.

One accountable contractor

No contractor in Florida self-performs every trade. The written scope names which elements we perform and which we coordinate, so it is clear who answers for what.

Groundwork before finish

Excavation, compaction, levels and drainage are where an outdoor project succeeds or fails. They are also the parts a cheap quote leaves vague — ask us about them.

An honest answer about fit

Sometimes the useful reply is that the work belongs to another trade, that a smaller scope would serve you better, or that we are not the right contractor for it.

Requirements confirmed, not assumed

How much approval a job needs depends on where it is and how big it is. Rather than guess, we put the question to whoever reviews the work in your jurisdiction once the scope is known.

Nothing invented

No fabricated review counts, no project photographs that are not ours, no statistics we cannot source. The projects and reviews pages explain what we will publish and when.
