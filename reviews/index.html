Reviews

What we will, and will not, publish

There is no review on this page, because there is no review we can attribute. Here is the standard instead — and six things you can check without taking our word for anything.

A testimonial wall is the cheapest thing on a contractor's website

Three glowing quotes, three plausible first names, three nearby cities. Fifteen minutes of work, no cost, and no way for you to check any of it. That is why almost every contractor has one, and why it tells you almost nothing.

The awkward consequence of taking that seriously is that we cannot show you ours. We have no review from a named customer who agreed to have it published, so there is nothing here to put in the cards. Writing something to fill the space would be the exact thing this page exists to argue against.

What follows instead: where we actually stand, what a review has to satisfy before it goes up, and — the useful part — six ways to check a contractor that do not depend on trusting a website. The Projects and Gallery pages apply the same rule to work and to photographs.

Where we stand

Nothing here is a review, and nothing here is a rating

Stated plainly, so you can hold the rest of this website to the same line.

0

Attributable customer reviews published

No star rating, no review count, no average score, and no quoted customer appears on this page. We hold no review we can trace to a real customer who consented to publication, so there is nothing legitimate to display.

This page also carries no review structured data. Search engines require that Review and aggregateRating markup describe reviews the visitor can actually see. Emitting it for reviews that do not exist would be a violation twice over — of the markup rules, and of the advertising rules that govern testimonials.

One thing you should know about the rest of this site

The homepage carries three testimonials and a rating figure that predate this rebuild. They came with the original design, not from a customer record, and they have not been verified. They are logged as the highest-risk open item in this project's specification and are flagged as a pre-launch blocker: confirm them against real reviews, or remove them. The same discipline is applied to work on the Projects page and to photography in the Gallery.

They are deliberately not reproduced here. Copying an unverified testimonial from one page to another does not make it more true; it doubles the exposure and makes it harder to retract. If you are reading this before that homepage section has been resolved, treat this page as the accurate one.

The standard

What a review has to carry before it appears here

Published now, while there is nothing to be tempted into bending it for. Every card that ever appears on this page can be checked against it.

  1. The customer's own words

    Unedited. Not tightened, not trimmed of hesitation, not rewritten into a stronger claim than the person made. If it needs a cut for length, the cut is marked.

  2. Real attribution

    A first name and last initial belonging to an actual customer, used with permission. No composites, no invented names, no "a homeowner in Weston".

  3. City, at the agreed level

    The customer decides how much identifies them, and can decline the city entirely. No street, no household-identifying date.

  4. The build type

    What the work actually was, so you can judge whether the review describes a project resembling yours. A driveway review is weak evidence about an outdoor kitchen.

  5. The source, linked

    Which platform it was left on, and a link to it where one exists, so you can read it where we cannot edit it. A quote we transcribe ourselves is worth less than one you can open.

  6. The critical ones too

    If a customer leaves a mixed or negative review, it stays as written. A page of unbroken praise tells a reader only that the filtering worked.

The rating comes last, not first

An average is a claim about a body of reviews. It gets published when there is a body of reviews to average, it gets calculated from the ones shown, and it moves when they do. A score printed above an empty page is not a summary of anything.

Due diligence

Six checks that beat any testimonial

All of them independent of this website, which is the point of listing them on it.

Verify the licence

Ours is CBC1269393. Florida licences are searchable through the state's Department of Business and Professional Regulation. It takes about a minute to confirm a number, its status, and who holds it.

Get the insurance certificate from the insurer

Not as a forwarded image. Check the dates cover your project and that the coverage matches the work. A certificate is easy to request and hard to fake at source.

Telephone the references

Ask any contractor for customers you can call, then call them. Ask what went wrong and how it was handled — every project of size has a moment, and the answer to that question is the whole review.

Walk a job in progress

A finished project shows you the taste. An open excavation shows you the base, the compaction, the drainage and how a crew keeps a site — which is what you are actually paying for.

Read the proposal for scope

Two quotes that differ by thousands usually differ by base depth, excavation, drainage or edge restraint — the paver installation page sets out what those layers actually do. The number on the front page is a summary of the specification behind it, and the specification is the part you are actually choosing between.

Read the one-star reviews first

Wherever you find them, for any contractor. Five-star reviews tell you what went right on an easy job. The low ones tell you what happens when something goes wrong, which is the information you are really buying.

On our Google profile

We hold no Google Business Profile URL to publish here, so this page links to none. Guessing at a search result and calling it our profile is how visitors end up reading about a different business with a similar name. Call or email and you will get the real link, or a straight answer that the profile is not set up yet.

Before you enquire

Questions about this page in particular

Why are there no reviews on your reviews page?

Because we have no review we can attribute to a real customer who agreed to have it published. Writing one is trivial and we are not going to. A testimonial wall costs a contractor nothing to produce and is therefore worth almost nothing as evidence, which is why this page offers you things you can check independently instead.

Do you have a star rating?

Not one we are willing to print. A rating is an arithmetic claim about a body of reviews, so publishing a score without the reviews behind it is worse than publishing nothing. If you find a number on any page of this site that is not backed by something you can verify, treat it as an error and tell us.

Can I see your Google Business Profile?

Ask us and we will send you the link. There is no Google profile URL in our records to publish here, and linking to a search result we have guessed at would be worse than linking to nothing — it is how visitors end up on the wrong business. Call or email and you will get the real address, or a straight answer that the profile is not set up yet.

How can I check you without reviews?

Verify the licence number, CBC1269393, through the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation — it is also on the about page with the rest of our contact detail. Request the certificate of insurance from the insurer rather than as a forwarded image. Ask for references you can telephone and for a job in progress you can walk. Then read the proposal for scope rather than price — two quotes that differ by thousands usually differ by base depth, excavation or drainage.

Will you ask me for a review?

We may ask, once, after the work is finished and you have lived with it. What we will not do is offer a discount, a rebate or anything else in exchange for one, make it a condition of anything, write it for you, or edit what you say into something stronger. If the review is critical it stays as written, because a page of unbroken praise tells a reader only that the critical ones were filtered.

What will a published review here actually contain?

The customer's own words, unedited. A real first name and last initial. The city, at the level the customer agreed to. The build type, so you can tell whether the review is about work resembling yours. The platform it came from and a link to it where one exists, so you can read it at source rather than taking our transcription on trust. Anything we cannot attribute does not go up.

Judge us on the proposal, not the praise

Ask for the licence, the insurance certificate, references you can call and a scope written in specifics. Then compare it against whatever else is on your table.
