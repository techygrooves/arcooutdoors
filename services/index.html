Grouped by how they get built, not by how they sell

Most contractor websites list services alphabetically. That is fine for a directory and useless for planning, because it hides the thing that actually governs your project: the order the work has to happen in.

So the eleven services below are grouped into four sets. Hardscape is everything with an excavated base under it — the layer every other outdoor element eventually sits on or beside. Outdoor living covers the structures and fittings that turn a paved area into somewhere you spend evenings, and those have footings and utilities that must be settled before the paving goes down. Yard and exterior is the frame: the ground cover, the boundary, and the openings between inside and out. Complete projects is what happens when several of the above are done together as one job.

If you already know which service you need, go straight to it. If you are weighing up two or three, the combining services section further down is the part worth reading first.

Category 01

Hardscape

The excavated, compacted and paved surfaces. These carry the loads, set the levels and decide where water goes, which is why they are almost always the first thing scheduled and the hardest thing to change later.

Paver Installation

The layer underneath most outdoor projects. Excavation, compacted sub-base, bedding, edge restraint and jointing — the parts nobody photographs, and the parts that decide whether a surface still sits flat in a few years.

Explore → paver installation

Patios

The outdoor room you actually use. Sizing it around dining, lounging and the route between house and garden matters more than square footage — an oversized slab with nowhere to sit is a common and expensive mistake.

Explore → patios

Driveways

The first thing anyone sees and the surface under the heaviest loads on the property. Pattern, edge restraint and base depth are all specified differently once a vehicle turns on a surface rather than walks across it.

Explore → driveways

Pool Decks

Wet feet, bare feet and pool chemistry make this the most demanding surface on a South Florida property. Slip resistance, how hot the material gets at midday and the coping detail at the water's edge outrank colour every time.

Explore → pool decks

Category 02

Outdoor Living

Shade and function. These are what make a paved area somewhere you spend an evening rather than somewhere you walk across, and they are the elements with footings and utilities that have to be positioned before paving.

Outdoor Kitchens

Counters, built-in grills, storage — and the services feeding them. Where power, water and gas run is a hardscape decision, not a kitchen decision, because those routes pass underneath whatever you pave.

Explore → outdoor kitchens

Pergolas

Shade that defines a room without enclosing it. A pergola is often what makes the difference between a patio used at seven in the evening and one used at two in the afternoon.

Explore → pergolas

Tiki Huts

Denser shade and a distinctly South Florida character. Position and footings are settled early — a hut dropped in after the paving is finished means cutting back into a surface that was just completed.

Explore → tiki huts

Category 03

Yard & Exterior

The frame around everything else: what covers the ground between the hard surfaces, what marks the boundary, and what separates inside from outside.

Artificial Turf

Green year-round with no mowing and no irrigation. In a climate with our rainfall, what happens under the turf — the base and how it drains — matters at least as much as the blade you choose.

Explore → artificial turf

Fencing

Boundary, privacy and the visual frame around everything you have built. Line and height are worth settling early, because a fence changes how enclosed a finished space feels far more than people expect.

Explore → fencing

Impact Windows & Doors

The connection between inside and out. Widening a glazed opening changes how an outdoor room gets used, because the two spaces stop being separate places you move between.

Explore → impact windows and doors

Category 04

Complete Projects

When several of the services above are done together, they stop being separate jobs and become one project with one sequence, one levels plan and one point of accountability.

Complete Outdoor Remodeling

A coordinated transformation rather than a list of bookings: paving, a shade structure, a kitchen, turf and a boundary planned together, sequenced in one order and finished as one space. It is the difference between a yard that looks assembled and one that looks designed.

Most of the value is invisible. Levels resolve once instead of three times. Drainage is planned across the whole site rather than surface by surface. Footings and service routes are set before anything is paved over.

Worth knowing

Why homeowners combine services

Nobody sets out to hire one contractor for the sake of it. The reasons people end up grouping work are practical, and they show up in the same four places every time.

Some work has to go underneath

Footings for a pergola or tiki hut, and the power, water or gas feeding an outdoor kitchen, all sit below or behind finished surfaces. Deciding on them after paving means cutting back into it. Deciding on them before costs nothing extra.

Levels and drainage are one problem

A patio, a pool deck and a turf lawn that meet each other have to agree on falls and finished heights. Planned together, water is directed once. Planned separately, you inherit whatever the previous surface decided.

Materials read as a set

Paving, coping, structure timber and fence line are seen together from the back door, not one at a time. Choosing them in the same conversation is the only reliable way to end up with a space that looks composed.

Mobilisation happens once

Excavation, machinery access, protection of existing planting and site clean-down are repeated for every separate contract. Doing related work in one visit removes that repetition — and the repeated disruption that comes with it.

None of this means you have to do everything at once

Phasing is normal, and often sensible. What matters is that the phases are planned against each other, so that phase one does not have to be partially undone to make phase two possible. If you know a pergola is likely in two years, that is worth saying now, even if you are only paving today.

How it runs

From an enquiry to a finished space

The same five stages whether the job is one driveway or a whole backyard. The difference is how much gets decided in stage two.

  1. Tell us the problem

    Not the specification — the problem. The yard bakes in the afternoon, the driveway lets the house down, there is nowhere to put twelve people. The brief follows from that.

  2. On-site visit

    Measuring the space, reading the falls, checking what is already in the ground and looking at how you move between house and garden. Material options are shown against the actual light and the actual surroundings.

  3. Scope and proposal

    What is included, in what order, with the materials named. If the work is phased, the phases are set out here, along with what each one leaves ready for the next.

  4. Build

    Excavation and base first, services and footings before anything covers them, then surfaces, then structures, then the soft finishes. Site cleaned down as work progresses rather than at the end.

  5. Walkthrough

    Walking the finished space together against what was approved, and going through how the materials that were installed should be looked after.

Before you enquire

Questions people ask when choosing a service

Can you do just one service, or do I have to remodel the whole yard?

Single services are normal work. Plenty of projects are one driveway or one patio and nothing else. The reason the services are grouped on this page is that some of them share a base, a levels plan or a drainage route — so if two of them are already on your list, it is worth knowing that before either one starts.

Which part of an outdoor project should come first?

Anything that goes under or through the ground comes before anything that sits on it. Footings for a pergola or tiki hut, and the services feeding an outdoor kitchen, are decided before paving is laid — because reaching them afterwards means lifting the surface you just paid for.

Can you work with hardscape that is already there?

Often, yes. Whether existing paving can be extended or built onto depends on how it was originally installed, what the base looks like underneath, and whether the levels still work for whatever you want to add. It is one of the first things looked at during the on-site visit, because the answer changes the scope significantly.

How are materials chosen?

By where the surface is and how it will be used, before appearance enters the conversation. A pool surround is judged on how it behaves wet and how hot it gets underfoot; a driveway is judged on how it takes turning loads. Colour, format and pattern are then chosen from whatever passes those tests — which is usually still a wide field.

What if I do not know what I want yet?

That is a normal starting point, and a better one than arriving with a half-formed specification. Most people describe a problem rather than a solution: the yard is unusable in the afternoon, the driveway looks tired, there is nowhere to seat everyone. The options follow from the problem.

Which areas of South Florida do you serve?

Broward, Miami-Dade and Palm Beach counties, working from Davie. Parkland, Davie, Weston, Plantation, Fort Lauderdale, Pembroke Pines, Coral Springs and Boca Raton are the markets we are asked for most often — the service areas page has the full list.
